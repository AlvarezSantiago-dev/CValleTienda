# Mostrador Claro

A visual philosophy for pieces that must be read at arm’s length and still feel meticulously crafted — the product of deep expertise, painstaking attention, and master-level execution.

Space is the first material. Margins are generous and unapologetic: a field of charcoal (`#0a0a09`) that lets a single typographic mass occupy the upper third without crowding. Form is architectural, not decorative — a vertical lime filete, a card with a quiet radius, a pill that sits like a tool on a counter. Nothing floats without a reason. Every alignment is the work of countless refinements.

Color is dual, not palettes. Carbón holds the room. Lima (`#65a30d`, highlight `#84cc16`) is the only accent: a question mark, a left edge on the proof card, the call-to-action pill. Material honesty: screenshots are photographs of real work, cropped with the same care as a printer’s proof, never dressed in fake devices. The lime is placed with the precision of a master craftsman, never as a wash, never as a gradient carnival.

Scale and rhythm follow the counter: one enormous question, then a pause, then a small proof. The hook is sculptural type — Instrument Sans, tight leading, three lines maximum — laborado as if each letter were kerned by hand. The proof sits smaller, subordinate, a window not a billboard. Quiet catalog marks (a pack number, a place name) whisper in tracked caps. The rhythm is: strike, rest, evidence.

Composition balances a heavy top with a grounded bar. The eye hits the question first, drops to the product as confirmation, lands on three words of action. Negative space between those three beats is not emptiness; it is the product of deep expertise in what to omit. Overlap is forbidden. Edges breathe. The bottom bar (place · pill) is a datum line, as considered as a shop counter’s front edge.

Hierarchy is hook → proof → mark. The wordmark is small, muted, present. The logo does not compete with the question. Text is sparse, essential-only, integrated as visual architecture: never a paragraph on the canvas. The final work must appear as though it took countless hours — meticulously crafted, labored over with care, master-level execution that a shop owner can read in one second and a designer would still respect.

This philosophy does not explain. It holds. It is reusable wherever a single human question must stand in a dark field and be believed.
